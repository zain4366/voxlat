# METHODS {#sec:methods}

## TPMS cell geometry {#sec:geom}

The gyroid (G) and Schwarz diamond (D) are represented by their nodal level sets [@vonschnering1991], normalized by their maxima so that both span [−1, 1]:

$$ \begin{aligned} G &= \tfrac{2}{3}\big(\sin X\cos Y + \sin Y\cos Z + \sin Z\cos X\big), \\ D &= \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big(\sin X\sin Y\sin Z + \sin X\cos Y\cos Z \\ &\qquad + \cos X\sin Y\cos Z + \cos X\cos Y\sin Z\big) \end{aligned} $$ {#eq:levelsets}

with $X = 2\pi\xi_x$ and $\xi$ the normalized cell coordinate. A blend $\phi_w = (1-w)\,G + w\,D$ interpolates between them. The solid is the network (skeletal) phase $\phi_w \le c(w,\rho^*)$, where the threshold $c$ is found by bisection so that the voxel solid fraction equals the target relative density $\rho^*$ (the continuum value is tabulated on a 21 × 99 grid in $(w,\rho^*)$ for fast evaluation). The network phase was chosen over the sheet phase because it leaves one connected fluid domain from inlet to outlet; a sheet TPMS splits the fluid into two disjoint labyrinths, the wrong topology for a single-coolant jacket. Fields are sampled at voxel centres, $\xi = (k + \frac12)/n$, so that tiling a cell reproduces the supercell exactly. A stretched cell $L \times L \times a_z L$ keeps cubic voxels and uses $\mathrm{round}(n a_z)$ voxels along $z$, so every downstream solver works with one voxel size. @fig:cells shows the three morphologies.

![fig:cells|col](figs/task1_tpms_cells.png) Network TPMS unit cells at ρ* = 0.30: gyroid (w = 0), Schwarz diamond (w = 1) and the 50/50 blend (w = 0.5); renders and slices with the voxel solid (grey) and the exact level set (line).

The geometry metrics are the specific surface area $a_{sf}$ (marching cubes on the periodic level set), the wall thickness (local thickness [@hildebrand1997] computed from sub-voxel distances to the surface) and the pore throat (the largest sphere that can travel through the fluid in all three directions). Applied to the zero level sets, the surface area per cell extrapolates to 3.0917 for G and 3.8381 for D, within 0.01% of the minimal-surface values 3.0915 and 3.8377 [@schroder2006]; the area converges at second order and is within 0.16% at n = 48. Because both the wall and the throat scale with $L$, the manufacturing limits reduce to a cell-size floor, $L \ge L_{min}(w,\rho^*)$. For the pure gyroid $L_{min}$ = 1.46–1.82 mm and for the diamond 1.77–2.16 mm, so almost the whole design box (L = 2–6 mm) is feasible. Blends behave differently: for 0 < w < 1 the minimum wall drops to 0.04–0.1 L at some densities because the blended level set develops pinch-point necks, and $L_{min}$ exceeds 6 mm on narrow "valleys" in the $(w,\rho^*)$ plane. Pointwise linear blending of two TPMS is therefore not a smooth morphological interpolation, a finding that shapes the optimization in @sec:rq2.

## Periodic cell problems {#sec:cell}

Three cell problems give the bulk closures. All are solved on the same binary voxel cell (true = solid) with periodic boundary conditions, and each solver computes its effective tensor in two independent ways (volume-averaged flux and energy) as a built-in consistency check.

**Conduction.** For each unit macroscopic gradient $\mathbf e_j$, the periodic temperature fluctuation $\vartheta_j$ solves

$$ \begin{aligned} &\nabla\cdot\left[k(\mathbf x)\left(\nabla\vartheta_j + \mathbf e_j\right)\right] = 0, \\ &k_{eff}\,\mathbf e_j = \left\langle k(\mathbf x)\left(\nabla\vartheta_j + \mathbf e_j\right)\right\rangle \end{aligned} $$ {#eq:cond}

with $k = k_s$ = 130 W m$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ in the solid and $k = k_f$ = 0.40 W m$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ in the coolant. Cell-centred finite volumes with harmonic-mean face conductances and a preconditioned conjugate-gradient solver (relative tolerance 10$^{-8}$) are used; the flux and energy forms agree to about 10$^{-9}$.

**Elasticity.** For each of the six unit macroscopic strains $\mathbf E_j$ (Voigt order, engineering shear), the periodic displacement fluctuation $\tilde{\mathbf u}_j$ solves

$$ \begin{aligned} &\nabla\cdot\left[\mathbb C(\mathbf x):\left(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\tilde{\mathbf u}_j) + \mathbf E_j\right)\right] = \mathbf 0, \\ &C_{eff}\,\mathbf E_j = \left\langle \mathbb C(\mathbf x):\left(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\tilde{\mathbf u}_j)+\mathbf E_j\right)\right\rangle \end{aligned} $$ {#eq:elast}

discretized with eight-node trilinear hexahedra on the solid voxels. The void is removed rather than given a small modulus: a soft void with $E_{min}$ biases $C_{eff}$ by about $22\,E_{min}/E_s$, whereas removal is exact and halves the cost. Stress localization is measured by the factor $K_\sigma = \sigma_{vm}/\Sigma_{vm}$ under unit macroscopic uniaxial and shear stresses. We use its 99th percentile, which is resolution-stable to ±3% from n = 24 upwards; the maximum is a voxel-corner singularity that keeps growing with resolution and is not used.

**Stokes permeability.** For a unit body force along $\mathbf e_j$, the creeping flow in the fluid voxels solves

$$ \begin{aligned} &\mu\nabla^2\mathbf u_j - \nabla p_j + \mathbf e_j = \mathbf 0, \quad \nabla\cdot\mathbf u_j = 0, \\ &\mathbf u_j = \mathbf 0 \ \text{on solid faces}, \quad K\,\mathbf e_j = \mu\left\langle\mathbf u_j\right\rangle \end{aligned} $$ {#eq:stokes}

with $\langle\cdot\rangle$ the average over the whole cell, so that $K$ is the superficial (Darcy) permeability. A staggered marker-and-cell discretization places the wall exactly on the voxel faces, as in the other two solvers, with mirror ghost velocities for tangential no-slip [@manwart2002]. The symmetric saddle-point system is solved with MINRES and a block-diagonal preconditioner [@silvester1994]. We report the symmetrized flux form of $K$, whose error is quadratic in the solver residual, and use the energy form as a check.

**Production estimator.** The voxel staircase drops edge and corner contacts between solid voxels, so all three quantities converge at about first order in $1/n$ and from below. The conductivity is the most affected (3–17% low on a single n = 32–64 grid, most at low density), the stiffness much less (−1.8 to −4.3% at n = 24 and −1.4 to −2.5% at n = 48), the permeability least (≤ 1.6% at n = 48). We therefore use the two-grid Richardson estimator [@roache1997]

$$ \begin{aligned} &q_\infty \approx \frac{n_2\,q(n_2) - n_1\,q(n_1)}{n_2 - n_1}, \\ &(n_1, n_2) = (32, 64) \end{aligned} $$ {#eq:richardson}

whose error against an extrapolated reference built from four grid offsets at n = 16–64 (and checked against n = 96 solutions) is at most 0.7% for $k_{eff}$, 2.9% for every stiffness constant and 1.0% for $K$ over gyroid and diamond at ρ* = 0.2–0.5.

**Verification.** @tbl:verif lists the verification cases; all pass. Homogeneous cells and laminates reproduce the exact (harmonic/arithmetic, Backus [@backus1962]) tensors to round-off, the conductivities lie inside the Hashin–Shtrikman bounds [@hashin1962], and the Stokes solver reproduces plane Poiseuille flow exactly (the MAC value $(H^2+2)/12$, i.e. a $2/H^2$ error against the continuum $H^2/12$), converges at second order for ducts and lies within 0.3–0.9% of the Zick–Homsy values for simple-cubic sphere arrays at six solid fractions [@zick1982] (@fig:stokesverif). As an independent validation, our gyroid permeability at porosities of 0.5 and 0.6 differs from the body-fitted OpenFOAM values of Gajetti et al. [@gajetti2025] by +1.1% and −1.8%, and the diamond values by +0.7% and −0.4%, i.e. agreement within the two-digit precision of their table.

## Closure dataset and surrogates {#sec:surr}

The device model needs the closures at every point of a graded field, so they are learned from a dataset. The morphology space $\theta = (w,\rho^*,a_z) \in [0,1]\times[0.20,0.50]\times[0.69,1.5]$ was sampled with 256 scrambled Sobol points [@owen1998], 8 corners, 12 edge midpoints and 14 points on the pure G and D lines (290 cells). Each cell was solved with the R(32, 64) estimators for $k_{eff}$, $C_{eff}$ and $K$, plus the geometry metrics and the localization factors at n = 64; the run took 7.4 h of wall-clock time on a laptop (27.8 CPU-h). Every cell had one connected, percolating solid and fluid.

To keep the predicted tensors symmetric positive-definite and their symmetry exact, the targets are the matrix logarithms of $K/L^2$, $k_{eff}/k_s$ and the Mandel-form $C_{eff}/E_s$ (the log-Euclidean metric [@arsigny2006]), plus the logs of $a_{sf}L$ and of the six 99th-percentile localization factors: 40 latent targets in all. The exponential of any symmetric prediction is SPD, and the material symmetry of each cell class (cubic for pure G and D, tetragonal for stretched pure cells, trigonal about [111] for blends, triclinic for stretched blends) is imposed by projection. Two surrogate families were trained on inputs $(w,\log\rho^*,\log a_z)$: an ARD Matérn-5/2 Gaussian process per target [@rasmussen2006] and a five-member heteroscedastic deep ensemble [@lakshminarayanan2017]. Their predictive standard deviations were recalibrated on out-of-fold errors [@kuleshov2018]. The cell size $L$ enters analytically ($K = (K/L^2)L^2$, $a_{sf} = (a_{sf}L)/L$).

In five-fold cross-validation the ensemble predicts the principal permeabilities with a mean absolute percentage error of 0.9%, the conductivity diagonal with 1.5%, the mean Young's modulus with 3.9%, the surface area with 0.2% and the localization factors with 2.9–3.5%; the recalibrated 95% intervals cover 86–98% of the held-out values. The errors concentrate in the pinch-neck region of the blends (0.3 < w < 0.7, ρ* < 0.4), where the mean Young's modulus error rises to 11.6% against 1.4% elsewhere. When all blends with 0.25 ≤ w ≤ 0.75 are withheld, the stiffness error reaches 43–49%: the neck mechanism is absent from the training data. A 3D convolutional network trained on 32³ voxel images did not extrapolate better across this gap (107% error), but it was the most accurate stiffness model when 25% of the blends were seen in training. The production device model uses the ensemble, with the GP as a cross-check.

## Finite-gap strips {#sec:gapmethod}

The jacket frame is $x = r$ (across the gap), $y = s$ (circumferential) and $z$ (axial). A strip is a lattice of $N$ cells across a gap $H = NL$ bounded by solid walls, periodic and one cell wide in $y$ and $z$. Because the cell solvers are periodic in $x$ too, the computational cell is the stack [lattice of thickness $H$ | solid wall of thickness $t_w = L/2$] repeated along $x$, so every lattice layer is bounded by a perfectly bonded no-slip wall of conductivity $k_s$ on both sides. The inner wall cuts the lattice at a phase $\varphi_0$ and the outer wall at $\varphi_0 + N$. For the gyroid and the diamond, the screw axes along $x$ make the cuts $\varphi_0$, $\varphi_0 + 1/4$ and $-\varphi_0$ equivalent, so the symmetry-distinct cuts lie in [0, L/8]; we use {0, L/12, L/8}. Blends have no such symmetry and are cut at three phases over a full period. The strip lattice is a bit-exact tiling of the bulk reference cell (same grid, phase and threshold), so the staircase error of @sec:cell is common to strip and bulk and cancels in their ratio; changing n from 24 to 48 changes the discrepancies by at most 0.4 percentage points (pp) for the permeability and 1.4 pp for the stiffness at N = 1.

The walls are then removed analytically under the homogenized picture "homogeneous lattice layer plus plain walls", which is exact for a laminate: the through-gap conductivity follows from the series sum, the in-plane permeability from the share of the flow carried by the lattice, and the stiffness from the laminate mixed form, in which the tractions $(\sigma_{xx},\sigma_{xz},\sigma_{xy})$ and the in-plane strains are continuous across layers [@backus1962]:

$$ \begin{aligned} &\frac{H + t_w}{k_{stack}} = \frac{H}{k_n} + \frac{t_w}{k_s}, \\ &K_t = \frac{H + t_w}{H}\,K_{stack}^{(yz)} \end{aligned} $$ {#eq:wallremoval}

Five apparent core properties are reported: the through-gap conductivity $k_n$, the in-plane permeability $K_t$ (mean of $K_{ss}$ and $K_{zz}$), the normal stiffness $C_{nn} = C_{rrrr}$, the transverse shear $G_t = (G_{rs} + G_{rz})/2$, which carries torque and thrust from the sleeve to the outer wall, and the in-plane shear $G_{sz}$. Their discrepancy from the homogenized prediction is

$$ \delta_q = \frac{q_{strip}}{q_{hom}} - 1 , $$ {#eq:delta}

where $q_{hom}$ is the bulk closure for uniform strips and, for graded strips, the local bulk closures combined across the gap like a laminate (series, mixed form or arithmetic mean), which is exactly what a local-closure device model does.

Each bonded wall adds a boundary layer whose effect does not depend on the distance to the other wall, so it should scale with the wall area per lattice volume, $2/H = 2/(NL)$. This suggests one wall coefficient $a$ per quantity, in a series form for $k_n$, $C_{nn}$, $G_t$ and a parallel form for $K_t$, $G_{sz}$:

$$ \frac{q_{bulk}}{q} = \left(1 + \frac{a(\theta)}{N}\right)\left(1 + c_g\,g^2\right)^{-1} $$ {#eq:gapser}

$$ \frac{q}{q_{bulk}} = \left(1 - \frac{a(\theta)}{N}\right)\left(1 + c_g\,g^2\right) $$ {#eq:gappar}

for the series quantities and the parallel ones, respectively, with $g = |\nabla\rho^*|L$ the density change per cell and $a(\theta) = \boldsymbol{\beta}\cdot[1,\, r,\, w,\, b,\, rw,\, rb,\, \ln a_z]$, $r = (\rho^*-0.35)/0.1$, $b = 4w(1-w)$, fitted by weighted least squares. In the parallel form, $aL/2$ is the thickness of a layer at each wall that carries no flow. A Gaussian process on the residuals tests whether anything systematic remains. Both models were judged by leave-one-$\theta$-out and leave-one-$N$-out cross-validation against the "floor", the pooled scatter over cut phases that no model in $(N,\theta,g)$ can remove. The study covers G, D and the w = 0.5 blend at ρ* = 0.25, 0.35, 0.45, N ∈ {1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8}, linear density gradients g ≤ 0.15 and stretches $a_z$ = 0.75 and 1.5: 336 strips and 157 bulk cells at n = 32 (92 min on two cores with a two-level aggregation preconditioner [@vanek1996]).

## Literature closures for inertia and interstitial heat transfer {#sec:lit}

Two closures were not computed: the inertial (Forchheimer) coefficient and the interstitial heat-transfer coefficient. With porosity $\phi = 1-\rho^*$, hydraulic diameter $D_h = 4\phi/a_{sf}$, superficial velocity $U$ and $Re_{D_h} = 4U/(a_{sf}\nu)$,

$$ \begin{aligned} -\nabla p &= \frac{\mu}{K}\mathbf U + \frac{\rho\,C_F}{\sqrt K}|\mathbf U|\mathbf U, \\ f\,Re_{D_h} &= \frac{2\phi D_h^2}{K} + 2\phi^2 C_F\frac{D_h}{\sqrt K}Re_{D_h} \end{aligned} $$ {#eq:forch}

with $f$ the Darcy friction factor based on the pore velocity [@savoldi2026]. After reviewing fourteen correlations (Supplementary Table S4) we use the power laws $C_F = b\,\phi^m$ of Gajetti et al. [@gajetti2025] (G: b = 0.0908, m = −1.81; D: b = 0.0666, m = −1.69; blends log-linear in w), the only source that covers G and D with one method, and the modified Reynolds analogy of Savoldi et al. [@savoldi2026] (their Eq. 37) extended to our Prandtl number with the Chilton–Colburn factor:

$$ Nu_{D_h} = \frac{h_{sf}D_h}{k_f} = 0.0267\,\phi^{0.19}\left(f\,Re_{D_h}\right)Pr^{1/3} $$ {#eq:nu}

evaluated with our own $K$, $a_{sf}$ and the Gajetti $C_F$. This variant has a constant Nusselt number in the Darcy limit and grows monotonically with Re across the jacket operating box. With $Pr^{1/3}$ it stays within 0.63–1.37 times the packed-bed correlation of Gnielinski [@vdi2010], which is valid up to Pr = 1000. Both closures are extrapolated: they were fitted for $Re_{D_h} \le 100$, ϕ ≤ 0.6–0.7 and Pr = 1, while the jacket operates at $Re_{D_h}$ = 25–577, ϕ = 0.5–0.8 and Pr = 20.7, where inertia carries 16–84% of the pressure drop. @sec:limits returns to this.

## Homogenized jacket model {#sec:device}

The annulus is unwrapped to the plane $(s,z)$, with $s = r_m\theta$ the arc length at the mean lattice radius $r_m$ = 74.5 mm, and every field is averaged over the gap depth. Curvature is neglected ($h/r_m$ = 0.08); the planform area times $h$ equals the annulus volume exactly. The cell axes coincide with $(r,s,z)$, so the predicted tensors are used directly; the in-plane permeability of a blend is the Schur complement $K_t = K_{tt} - K_{tr}K_{rr}^{-1}K_{rt}$ of the full tensor (zero net radial flow), which keeps its off-diagonal $K_{sz}$.

**Flow.** The superficial velocity $\mathbf U = (U_s, U_z)$ obeys the depth-averaged Darcy–Forchheimer equation with the finite-gap factor on the viscous term only:

$$ \begin{aligned} -\nabla p &= \mu\left(f_K K_t\right)^{-1}\mathbf U + \frac{\rho\,C_F}{\sqrt{K_e}}|\mathbf U|\mathbf U, \qquad \nabla\cdot\mathbf U = 0, \\ f_K &= 1 - a_K(\theta)/N, \qquad N = h/L \end{aligned} $$ {#eq:darcy}

with $K_e = 1/(\mathbf e\cdot K_t^{-1}\mathbf e)$ the bulk permeability along the local flow direction $\mathbf e$. The manifold slots are plenums at uniform pressure ($p_{in}$, unknown, and 0); the total flow rate fixes $p_{in}$; the axial ends are no-flow. Cell-centred finite volumes with harmonic face mobilities and an implicit cross term for anisotropic cells are used, and the inertial term is linearized by Picard iteration (3 iterations for uniform designs, 10–15 for graded ones).

**Heat.** A two-equation (local thermal non-equilibrium) model [@nield2017] is reduced to three depth-averaged fields: the coolant temperature $T_f$, the sleeve temperature $T_w$ and the mean lattice-solid temperature $\bar T_s$:

$$ \rho c_p h\,\mathbf U\cdot\nabla T_f - \nabla\cdot\left(h\phi k_f\nabla T_f\right) = U_{eff}\left(T_w - T_f\right) $$ {#eq:fluid}

$$ \begin{aligned} &-\nabla\cdot\left(k_w t_{sl}\nabla T_w\right) - \nabla\cdot\left(h\,\mathbf k_t\nabla\bar T_s\right) \\ &\qquad + U_{eff}\left(T_w - T_f\right) = q_{in}(z) \end{aligned} $$ {#eq:wall}

Across the gap, the lattice solid is a one-dimensional fin bonded to the sleeve with an adiabatic outer wall, which gives

$$ \begin{aligned} U_{eff} &= \sqrt{k_n H_v}\,\tanh(mh) + \phi\,h_{sf}, \quad m = \sqrt{H_v/k_n}, \quad H_v = h_{sf}a_{sf}, \\ \bar T_s &= T_f + \eta\left(T_w - T_f\right), \qquad \eta = \tanh(mh)/(mh) \end{aligned} $$ {#eq:fin}

where the second term of $U_{eff}$ is the direct convection from the exposed sleeve fraction ϕ (the planar porosity at a cut equals the bulk porosity, @sec:rq1). The in-plane solid conductivity $\mathbf k_t = (k_{ss}, k_{zz})$ comes from the surrogate; $k_n = k_{eff,rr}$ needs no finite-gap correction (@sec:rq1). The heat input $q_{in}(z) = q''(z)\,r_s/r_m$ is integrated exactly over each cell, so its sum is 770 W. Under a manifold slot there is no lattice; the sleeve exchanges heat with the well-mixed plenum through $h_m$, taken from fully developed laminar flow between parallel plates with one wall heated, $Nu_{D_h}$ = 5.385 with $D_h = 2h$ [@shah1978], about 180 W m$^{-2}$ K$^{-1}$ (a conservative value; see the sensitivity study in @sec:device_results). Advection is first-order upwind and the axial ends are adiabatic. The reported wall temperature is that of the stator side of the sleeve, $T_{wall} = T_w + q''t_{sl}/k_s$.

**Structure.** The sleeve and the outer wall are treated as rigid faces of a cylindrical sandwich whose lattice core carries the torque $T$ and thrust $F$ in shear with uniform shear strain. The local shear stresses at the sleeve radius $r_i$ follow the local transverse moduli,

$$ \begin{aligned} \tau_{rs} &= \frac{T\,G_{rs}}{r_m\sum G_{rs}A}\left(\frac{r_m}{r_i}\right)^2, \\ \tau_{rz} &= \frac{F\,G_{rz}}{\sum G_{rz}A}\,\frac{r_m}{r_i} \end{aligned} $$ {#eq:sandwich}

the coolant pressure loads the lattice in radial tension, shared with the hoop stiffness of the outer wall, and the local 99th-percentile von Mises stress is the linear superposition $\sigma_{vm} = K_{ux}\sigma'_{rr} + \sqrt3\,(K_{s,xy}\tau_{rs} + K_{s,xz}\tau_{rz})$ with the localization factors of @sec:cell. The structural margin is $\sigma_{allow}/\sigma_{vm}$, minimized over the lattice and the outer wall (hoop stress, strip bending over a slot and plate bending over a pore).

**Verification.** The model reproduces uniform Darcy and Darcy–Forchheimer flow against the analytic two-path solution to $2\times10^{-13}$, layered media to 10$^{-10}$, the exact discrete upwind solution of the ε-NTU problem at fixed wall temperature to 10$^{-12}$ (and the continuous ε-NTU solution at first order), and the analytic fin conductance to 10$^{-7}$. Mass and energy balances close to better than $10^{-13}$ and $2\times10^{-11}$ of the throughput on graded, blended and stretched designs at 1 and 5 L/min, far inside the 0.5% target. The thermal resistance converges at an observed order of 1.76; on the default grid (1.25 mm × 2.5 mm, 376 × 20 cells) it is 0.44% above the Richardson-extrapolated value (@fig:deviceverif). One evaluation takes 0.2 s for uniform and 0.3–0.7 s for graded designs on one CPU core.

## Baselines {#sec:baselines}

Four baselines are evaluated with the same interface, heat input, sleeve conduction and wall-temperature definition, and each is tuned before comparison:

- **B1**, helical rectangular channels in the same 6 mm gap with end-ring manifolds outside the heated length. Channel width, rib thickness (≥ 0.35 mm) and number of starts were searched over 479 candidates. Friction follows the exact laminar series for rectangular ducts [@shah1978] with Schmidt's curvature factor [@schmidt1967], and the Nusselt number follows the VDI laminar coil correlation, floored by the straight-duct value, with Gnielinski's transition and turbulent forms [@gnielinski1986; @vdi2010]. Ribs are fins with an adiabatic tip, the same outer-wall assumption as for the lattice.
- **B1s**, circumferential channels fed by the same axial slots as the lattice (75 candidates), the like-for-like channel baseline.
- **B2**, the best uniform gyroid (221 candidates in ρ* = 0.20–0.50, L = 2–6 mm).
- **B3**, density-only grading of a gyroid at fixed cell size, emulating prior art: four density knots along each half path plus an axial term shaped like $q''(z)$, tuned by 64 Sobol points and a Nelder–Mead search.

All families use the same selection rule: minimum R at 3 L/min subject to manufacturability, structural margin ≥ 1 and a pump power no higher than that of the reference uniform gyroid (ρ* = 0.35, L = 4 mm), 0.306 W.

## Multi-objective optimization {#sec:opt}

Each design field $f \in \{\rho^*, w, L\}$ is a tensor-product B-spline on the unwrapped jacket,

$$ \begin{aligned} f(s,z) &= f_{lo} + \left(f_{hi} - f_{lo}\right)\sum_{i,j} x_{ij}\,B_i^{s}(s)\,B_j^{z}(z), \\ x_{ij} &\in [0,1] \end{aligned} $$ {#eq:bspline}

with a periodic cubic basis in $s$ and a clamped basis in $z$. Because the bases are non-negative partitions of unity, the fields never leave their bounds. The default control grids are 8 × 4 for ρ* and w and 4 × 2 for L, with $a_z$ = 1: 72 variables. The problem is

$$ \begin{aligned} &\min_{\mathbf x}\ \left(R,\ P,\ m\right) \ \text{at 3 L/min, subject to} \\ &L \ge 1.01\,L_{min}(w,\rho^*) \ \text{in every cell}, \\ &|\nabla\rho^*|\,L \le 0.05, \qquad \sigma_{allow}/\sigma_{vm} \ge 1 \end{aligned} $$ {#eq:problem}

The gradient limit of 0.05 is the value at which the graded strips of @sec:rq1 keep the local-closure error below 1%. The cell-size rule uses a morphology table with a blend step of 0.025 and excludes the pinch-neck valleys (cells whose neighbourhood in the table has a minimum wall below 0.09 L). A first study with a coarser table let optimizers place blends on these valleys, which a direct n = 48 check later rejected; the corrected rule is used throughout this paper. Random B-spline fields are averages of several controls and therefore mostly mid-range blends: only 8 of 40 000 Sobol designs were feasible. A deterministic repair, applied identically by every method, polarizes violating w controls toward the design's majority pure type, raises the cell-size controls where needed and shrinks the density net toward its mean until the gradient limit holds; it maps every random design to a feasible one in 3 ms and leaves feasible designs unchanged. Because the extremes of the front lie where every field is at a bound, every method starts from the same nine uniform anchors (G and D at the four (ρ*, L) corners and the reference gyroid), counted in its budget.

Three methods were run with five seeds on each of the two manifold layouts: NSGA-II [@deb2002] in pymoo [@blank2020] (population 100, 100 generations, 10 000 evaluations); batch Bayesian optimization with the log-form qNEHVI acquisition [@daulton2021; @ament2023] in BoTorch [@balandat2020] (200 evaluations in batches of four; the mass, a known linear function of the density controls, is not modelled by a GP); and scrambled Sobol search (1000 evaluations). An evaluation is one call of the device model for a design that passed the cheap checks. Fronts are compared by the hypervolume [@emmerich2006] in a fixed normalized objective space (R in mK W$^{-1}$ on [5, 40], $\log_{10}P$ on [−3, 0.5], mass on [0.28, 0.42] kg) with the reference point (1, 1, 1). The 30 runs were executed in parallel on 4-core cloud runners (2 h 35 min wall clock in total).

## Geometry export and manufacturability checks {#sec:export}

A chosen design is turned into a solid with the PicoGK geometry kernel [@picogk]. The graded fields are mapped into conformal cylindrical phase coordinates so that cells never tear: an integer number of cells around the circumference, $N_\theta = \mathrm{round}(\overline{P}(2\pi))$ with $P(\theta, z) = \int_0^\theta r_m/L\,\mathrm d\theta'$, the axial phase $u_z = \int dz/(a_z L)$ and the radial phase $u_r = \varphi_{r0} + (r_m/L)\ln(r/r_i)$, which keeps the cell shape constant through the gap. The blended level set of Eq. (@eq:levelsets) is evaluated at these coordinates in a C# implicit function, intersected with the gap, and united with the sleeve, outer wall, end caps, plenums and port bosses. A pure-numpy mirror of the same signed-distance function serves as the specification and as an independent check. The checks run on voxel masks of a 30° sector at 0.05 mm and of the full jacket at 0.2 mm: minimum wall by a discrete opening, pore throat by the largest sphere that passes from inlet to outlet, fluid connectivity between the ports, trapped pockets and loose solid.
